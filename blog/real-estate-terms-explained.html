Philippine Real Estate Terms, Explained
Property listings and loan paperwork throw around a lot of terms that sound similar but aren't. Here's what the ones people mix up most actually mean.
Home & Condo September 19, 2026

1. Land vs. built space

Lot area is the size of the land parcel itself, in square meters — the ground beneath the structure plus any yard, garden, or driveway.

Floor area is the indoor living space of the structure — add up every story's floor space and that's your floor area. A 2-story house on a 100 sqm lot easily has more floor area than lot area, since the upper floor counts again.

Listings usually show both, e.g. "120 sqm lot / 90 sqm floor area." Confusing the two is the single most common reason a buyer misjudges how big a property actually feels in person.

2. House configurations

Single attached — one side of the house sits directly on the property line, usually sharing a firewall with the neighboring unit. Maximizes lot usage, which is why it's common in subdivisions built for affordability.

Single detached — the house stands in the middle of the lot with open space on all sides. More privacy, room for a garden, but a bigger lot is needed for the same floor area.

Same underlying configurations, different framing for different purposes.

3. Property titles — your actual ownership document

TCT (Transfer Certificate of Title) — the primary title document for land or house-and-lot properties. Names the registered owner and describes the land itself.

CCT (Condominium Certificate of Title) — the condo equivalent. Covers your specific unit plus your proportional share of the building's common areas (lobby, hallways, amenities, the land the building sits on).

Before you sign anything, verify the title is clean at the Registry of Deeds — no existing liens, mortgages, or adverse claims. This is standard due diligence regardless of financing type, and something a bank or Pag-IBIG will also check before releasing a loan.

4. Down payment vs. equity — these get used interchangeably, and that causes real confusion

Down payment is the upfront portion of the price you pay before the loan covers the rest. Who actually sets the minimum depends on financing type: for bank and Pag-IBIG loans, it's driven by the lender's requirement (commonly 20% is treated as standard, sometimes as low as 10% for certain Pag-IBIG brackets); for developer in-house financing, the developer sets it directly, often 20–30% of the total contract price, frequently payable in installments over months before the loan portion is even released.

Equity, in a Philippine real-estate sales context, is often used as a synonym for that same down payment — "monthly equity" commonly means the installment payments toward your down payment. But equity has a second, different meaning too: your ownership stake — property value minus whatever you still owe on the loan, which grows every year as you pay down principal and (usually) as the property appreciates.

Both senses are correct, just different contexts. When a developer says "low monthly equity," they mean your down payment installments. When KayaKo's simulator shows "Your Equity at Year 10," that's the ownership-stake sense — what you'd actually walk away with if you sold. Worth knowing which one a conversation means before assuming.

5. Sales agreements — CTS vs. DOAS

Contract to Sell (CTS) — the seller promises to sell and the buyer promises to buy, but the title stays with the seller until the purchase price is fully paid. This is the agreement you sign first, especially on installment/in-house financing.

Deed of Absolute Sale (DOAS) — executed once every condition is met, usually full payment. This is the document that actually transfers the title from seller to buyer, and what gets presented to the Registry of Deeds to issue a new TCT or CCT in your name.

In short: CTS is the promise, DOAS is the transfer. If a loan is involved, the DOAS typically gets executed once the bank or Pag-IBIG releases the loan proceeds to the seller.
